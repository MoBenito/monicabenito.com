Light and matter exchange energy and information one quantum at a time. When a single electron spin sits close to a superconducting resonator, which confines microwave photons to a tiny volume, this exchange can become faster than any loss. In this regime of strong coupling, spin and photon merge into one hybrid quantum system.

Such hybrid light–matter systems give spin qubits something they lack on their own: a way to talk over long distances. A photon in a resonator can carry quantum information between spins that sit far apart on a chip, mediate two-qubit gates through virtual photons, and read out the qubit state without destroying it.

My interests in this field range from the theory of spin–photon coupling in silicon and new platforms such as electrons on solid neon, over several cavities coupled to one spin ensemble, to the fundamental question of how the physics of classically driven systems emerges from fully quantized light–matter interaction. Optomechanical systems, where light couples to the vibrations of a membrane, add yet another flavour.
