Pauli spin blockade, the standard spin readout in semiconductor qubits, seen as polarimetry with spins.

Polarimetry, the rotation of light's polarization by optically active media, has driven discoveries from astronomy to medicine. We recast Pauli spin blockade, the main spin-readout mechanism in semiconductor quantum computers, as its natural extension to spins. Using a silicon quantum dot exchanging a hole with a boron acceptor, we show how spin-orbit coupling misaligns the spins and how rotating the magnetic field restores alignment. Spin misalignment sets a fundamental upper limit on readout fidelity in qubits read out via spin blockade.
