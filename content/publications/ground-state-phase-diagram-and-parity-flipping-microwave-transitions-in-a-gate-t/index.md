Microwave spectroscopy of a gate-tunable InAs nanowire Josephson junction reveals unexpected parity-flipping transitions.

We couple a gate-tunable InAs nanowire Josephson weak link to a microwave resonator. Close to pinch-off, the ground state alternates between a singlet and a doublet as the gate voltage or the superconducting phase difference changes. Near the singlet-doublet boundaries, the microwave absorption spectra show lines approaching zero energy, suggesting parity-flipping transitions that are normally forbidden in microwave spectroscopy. We tentatively explain them with an ancillary state in the junction acting as a reservoir for single electrons.
